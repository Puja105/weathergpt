# Understanding India's colour-coded weather warnings

Source: Ministry of Earth Sciences, Government of India, parliamentary answer on IMD warnings.
Source URL: https://www.moes.gov.in/sites/default/files/LS-English-3461-22032023.pdf
Reference captured: 2 November 2026.

The India Meteorological Department (IMD) uses colour-coded warnings to communicate
the expected impact of severe weather and the preparedness/action expected.

## Warning colours

- **Green — No warning:** no warning is in effect; no special action is indicated by
  the colour code.
- **Yellow — Watch / be aware:** stay watchful and keep checking updated information.
- **Orange — Alert / be prepared to take action:** prepare for possible impacts and
  follow official advisories.
- **Red — Warning / take action:** take the actions instructed by local authorities
  and official warnings.

The colour indicates an action level, not a measured probability of rain. The
specific hazard, district, validity period, and impact statement in the latest
official bulletin matter. Do not infer a warning colour from rainfall values in
WeatherGPT's forecast tables.

IMD's Impact Based Forecasting approach describes expected impacts and public
guidance, not only the weather event itself. Always check the latest IMD bulletin
for location-specific and time-specific warnings.

## How WeatherGPT should explain warnings

1. State the warning colour and hazard only when that information is present in an
   official, current warning source.
2. Mention the affected district/area and the warning's validity period when supplied.
3. Explain the action level in plain language.
4. Do not claim that a city has an active IMD alert based only on forecast rain values.
5. If current official warning data is unavailable, say that the live alert status
   could not be verified and direct the user to the official IMD website.
